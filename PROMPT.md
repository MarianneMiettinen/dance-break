# Dance Break — the improved prompt

> Stage 0 deliverable. This is the spec the build works from.
> Merged from MM's original prompt and her two-step user flow.

---

## What it is, in one sentence

A desk-side movement break: at times **you set**, it offers one dance move, plays a
beat in time with it, shows the move on the count — and if you danced for three
minutes, puts a medal in your cupboard.

## Who it's for

Persona **A** (`audience.md`) — early career, ADHD, low capacity, high self-judgment,
mid-avoidance at the moment of contact. Declared rather than asked; change one line in
`CLAUDE.md` if wrong.

Target state, from audience.md: **ends the interaction feeling capable and in control
of their time.** Not "exercised". Not "compliant".

---

## The flow

### Step 1 — set it up

Open the app. Add a time, pick the days it runs. Each row has **Remove**; the list has
**Clear all**. Nothing is required — **Dance now** works on a cold open with no setup
at all.

Above the list sits the pacing advice, and it **responds to what is already set**
rather than repeating one fixed line:

| Slots per week | What it says |
|---|---|
| 0 | Start with one break a week. Once that one sticks by itself, add another. |
| 1–2 | One or two a week is a good place to stay for a while. |
| 3–5 | That is a rhythm. Add more only if these are already happening. |
| 6+ | That is a lot of breaks. Keeping the ones that stick beats adding more. |

*Why it changes:* a fixed "start slow" line becomes wallpaper by the third read, and
worse, it keeps telling someone with six breaks a week to start slow. Advice that
notices what you did is advice; advice that repeats itself is decoration.

### Step 2 — the break

Time arrives → the app comes forward and offers the move → you press play → figure,
beat, and counted cues run together → you leave with **Continue**, or keep going.

**Dance three minutes or more, and you get a reward** when you press Continue or when
the break ends. Medal every time; **every fifth is a statue instead**, frozen in the
move you earned it with. Everything goes to the **cupboard**, reachable from home, from
the reward screen, and from the break screen.

## Decisions that changed the original prompt

### 1. "Reminds me" — a web page cannot open itself

**Decision:** ship the same code twice. Plain web app now, MV3 Chrome extension at
Stage 2 so `chrome.alarms` fires with no tab open. One codebase, two shells, and
`js/schedule.js` is the seam.

**Honest limit today:** Stage 1 can only come forward if a tab is already open. If the
tab is in front it takes over; if it is behind it fires a notification and waits rather
than ambushing you. **"The app opens itself" is genuinely Stage 2 work** — that is the
main reason Stage 2 is next.

### 2. "Plays music" — three problems at once

Browsers block audio before a user gesture; audience.md bans autoplay independently;
and royalty-free tracks mean either bundled files to licence-audit or a network request.

**Decision:** the music is **generated by the Web Audio API** — drums plus bass, one
pattern per style, at that style's BPM. No licence to audit, no bytes shipped, no
network, works offline, and it shares a clock with the animation so the move is
*always* on the beat. Playback starts on a tap, never before.

*Cost, named:* it is a groove, not a song. A file-backed player drops into the same
interface later if you want real tracks.

### 3. "Shows the moves" / "plays the video" — REVERSED on 2026-09-12

**Original decision:** no video; an SVG figure with counted cues, because video cannot be
counted or mirrored.

**MM's correction:** *"it needs to host real videos (15) because the instructions don't
make any sense."* She is right, and the reasoning was backwards: written steps plus a
stick figure are a fine *supplement* to seeing a person do the move, and a poor
*substitute* for it. The count matters, but not before you know what the thing looks like.

**What shipped instead:** both, as a toggle on the break screen.

| Mode | What it is | When it wins |
|---|---|---|
| **Watch it** (default) | A real tutorial, embedded | Learning the move at all |
| **Count it out** | The animated figure + generated beat | Practising to a count; works offline |

The figure was not wasted — it still draws every statue in the cupboard, frozen in the
move it was earned with.

**Videos loop.** Eleven of the fifteen tutorials are shorter than three minutes, so
watching once could never reach the break length. Looping is also simply what you want
while practising a step.

### 4. "Royalty-free or YouTube" — verified, not guessed

All 15 were verified, three separate ways, because they answer three different questions:

| Check | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| oEmbed returns HTTP 200 | Does the video exist and is it public? |
| `YT.Player` fires `onReady`, not `onError` 101/150 | Does the owner permit embedding? |
| Aspect ratio is landscape | Is it a vertical Short that would letterbox? |

All 15 passed all three. `node tools/check-videos.mjs` re-runs the existence check on
demand and exits non-zero if one has gone, because videos rot.

Three channels cover multiple moves each — Howcast (3), Bon Sueno (2), Bustamovebook (3) —
which gives the set some visual consistency.

**Privacy cost, named:** the app previously made zero network requests. It now loads the
YouTube IFrame API from youtube.com and the player from youtube-nocookie.com. That is a
real change and the footer copy was rewritten to say so. Nothing loads until a break opens.

### 5. Rewards — built as a collection, not a streak

`audience.md` rules out streaks, milestones and progress comparisons because
*"they've already failed"* is triggered by exactly those. A reward system sits close to
that line, so it is built on the side of it that does not bite:

- **Nothing is ever removed.** A collection accumulates. A streak breaks. There is no
  state in which the cupboard gets worse.
- **Deterministic materials, never random.** You can see what the next one will be, on
  the cupboard screen, in words. A variable-ratio reward is a slot machine.
- **No empty slots, no "3 of 50", no missed count.** The cupboard shows what you have.
- **Under three minutes is never framed as a loss** — "Nothing to log. Your legs know
  you did it."

Medal materials cycle bronze → copper → silver → jade → sky → coral → slate →
amethyst → pearl → gold. Statues cycle bronze → marble → jade → amethyst → gold →
obsidian → copper → silver.

## Content

3 styles x 5 moves = 15. Every move is doable in **one square metre beside a desk, in
socks, without jumping**, and learnable in under 30 seconds.

| Style | BPM | Moves |
|---|---|---|
| Hip-Hop Groove | 92 | Two-Step, Bounce, Heel-Toe Rock, Shoulder Roll, Grapevine |
| Latin Step | 96 | Side Basic, Merengue March, Back Rock, Cumbia Step, Basic Step |
| Disco / Funk | 116 | The Point, The Hustle, Step-Touch Clap, Arm Roll, The Bump |

One move per break. All five in a style come round before any repeats.

## Behaviour rules — non-negotiable

- **Works on first open.** No signup, no onboarding, no config before the first useful
  moment.
- **Continue is always there**, never buried, never guilt-worded. No red anywhere.
- **~12 words per screen element.** One idea per screen.
- Never imply willpower, discipline, effort or laziness. Never "just".
- The schedule is the user's. They set it, remove it, clear it, switch it off.
- WCAG 2.1 AA floor: contrast, visible focus, 44px targets, full keyboard path,
  Escape leaves any screen.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: decorative motion off, tweening off — the figure snaps
  pose-to-pose on the beat, and the written steps carry the move alone.

## Privacy

No network requests at all. Your times, styles and cupboard live in `localStorage` and
nowhere else. No account, no analytics. The "See it danced" link is the only thing that
ever leaves the machine, and only when clicked.

### Known gaps in Stage 1, stated plainly

- The app cannot open itself with no tab open. Stage 2 fixes this; it is the point of
  Stage 2.
- Reduced-motion is implemented but **not yet tested** under an actual
  `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` setting.
- Contrast ratios are chosen by eye against the AA floor, **not yet measured**. Stage 3.
- The 15 poses are tuned from the contact sheet, not from anyone dancing them. They
  will need a pass with a real person in front of a screen.
